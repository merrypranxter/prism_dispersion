# prism_dispersion — REPO SEED

## Type
Spectral generator (raymarched per-wavelength refraction). Heaviest of the four.

## The Physics

### Snell's Law
```
n₁·sin(θ₁) = n₂·sin(θ₂)
```
- n₁ = IOR of incident medium (air ≈ 1.0)
- n₂ = IOR of refracting medium (glass, water, diamond)
- θ₁ = angle of incidence
- θ₂ = angle of refraction

For a given wavelength λ, the refracted angle is:
```
sin(θ₂) = (n₁/n₂(λ)) · sin(θ₁)
```

### Cauchy Dispersion
```
n(λ) = A + B/λ²  (λ in µm)
```
- A = base refractive index (material property)
- B = dispersion coefficient (material property)
- λ = wavelength in micrometers

Typical values:
- Crown glass: A ≈ 1.52, B ≈ 0.004 µm²
- Flint glass: A ≈ 1.66, B ≈ 0.01 µm² (high dispersion)
- Diamond: A ≈ 2.42, B ≈ 0.004 µm² (high IOR + moderate dispersion = "fire")
- Water: A ≈ 1.33, B ≈ 0.001 µm² (low dispersion, weak rainbow)

### Dispersion Example (Crown Glass)
- 380nm (violet): n = 1.52 + 0.004/(0.38)² = 1.52 + 0.0277 = 1.5477
- 550nm (green): n = 1.52 + 0.004/(0.55)² = 1.52 + 0.0132 = 1.5332
- 700nm (red): n = 1.52 + 0.004/(0.70)² = 1.52 + 0.0082 = 1.5282
- Difference: Δn = 0.0195 → blue bends ~2.5% more than red

### Raymarching Glass
1. Cast ray from camera through pixel
2. Find intersection with glass SDF (prism, sphere, gem, blob)
3. At entry: refract ray by n(λ) for each wavelength sample
4. Trace refracted ray through glass interior
5. At exit: refract again by n(λ) (Snell's law, exiting)
6. For each λ, accumulate the path color (transmission + absorption)
7. Also compute reflection (Fresnel) at each interface
8. Sum all λ contributions → spectrum → RGB

### Fresnel Reflection (Schlick Approximation)
```
R(θ) = R₀ + (1 - R₀)·(1 - cos(θ))⁵
R₀ = ((n₁ - n₂)/(n₁ + n₂))²
```
- At grazing angles, reflection → 1 (total reflection)
- At normal incidence, reflection = R₀
- For glass-air: R₀ ≈ 0.04 (4% reflection)
- For diamond-air: R₀ ≈ 0.17 (17% reflection) — much more sparkle

### Total Internal Reflection (TIR)
When exiting a high-IOR material to low-IOR (e.g., diamond to air):
- If sin(θ₂) > 1, TIR occurs — ray reflects instead of refracting
- This is the source of diamond's internal sparkle
- Critical angle: θ_c = arcsin(n₂/n₁) = arcsin(1/2.42) ≈ 24.4° for diamond

### Dispersive Caustics
Light through a dispersive medium creates caustics with rainbow fringes:
- Each wavelength focuses at a slightly different point
- The caustic pool is a superposition of differently-colored focal points
- Can be approximated by photon mapping or by accumulating many raymarched samples

## Per-Wavelength Raymarching Algorithm

For each pixel:
1. Cast primary ray from camera
2. For each wavelength sample λ in [380, 700]nm (N samples):
   a. Compute n(λ) via Cauchy
   b. Raymarch glass SDF, find entry point
   c. Refract ray at entry: sin(θ₂) = sin(θ₁)/n(λ)
   d. Trace through glass interior
   e. Refract at exit: sin(θ₂) = n(λ)·sin(θ₁)
   f. Accumulate transmission color (with Beer-Lambert absorption)
   g. Compute reflection at each interface (Fresnel)
   h. Add reflected contribution (environment map or sky color)
3. Build spectrum S(λ) from all wavelength contributions
4. Convert to RGB via spectral_color.spectrumToRGB()
5. Post-process: bloom, tone mapping, vignette

## Aesthetic Regimes

1. **dark_side**: Single white beam, triangular prism, classic spectrum fan on pure black. The iconic Pink Floyd cover. Minimalist, high contrast.
2. **diamond_fire**: Faceted gem (octahedron or brilliant cut), rotating slowly. TIR sparkle, dispersive flashes. High IOR (2.4), moderate dispersion. The "fire" is the rainbow sparkle from internal reflections.
3. **liquid_glass**: A wobbling, morphing glass blob (metaball SDF). A scene or `u_source` image is refracted through it with dispersive edges. The blob deforms over time, creating shifting rainbow fringes.
4. **spectral_caustics**: Light through a glass prism casts rainbow-fringed caustic pools on a floor. The pools shift and shimmer as the light source moves. Each pool is a focal point where different wavelengths converge differently.

## Visual Targets
- Dark side: sharp triangular prism, pure white beam entering, vivid spectrum fan exiting, black background. The spectrum should be smooth, not banded, with all colors visible.
- Diamond fire: brilliant-cut diamond, rotating, internal rainbow flashes from TIR. The diamond should sparkle — bright white highlights plus rainbow internal reflections. Dark background, high contrast.
- Liquid glass: a transparent, wobbling blob distorting a background image. The edges of the blob have rainbow fringes (dispersion). The blob should feel like liquid glass — thick, viscous, slow-moving.
- Spectral caustics: bright light source, glass object, floor with rainbow caustic pools. The pools should be sharp and colorful, not blurry. The floor should be light-colored (white or gray) to show the caustics clearly.
